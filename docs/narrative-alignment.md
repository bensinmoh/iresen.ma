The seven research themes describe technological questions; they are distinct

## Editorial lessons for every future content item

Start with the need and the people or organisations concerned. Explain IRESEN's
contribution, the work, the partners' roles and what the results enable. Complete
the account with evidence, the actual stage and what is learned from use.

- Make the value for public institutions, businesses, researchers, territories
  and international partners explicit. Industrial decarbonisation, energy
  performance and Moroccan/regional application conditions are needs and context;
  they do not establish achieved effects.
- Explain infrastructure through research, testing, demonstration, training and
  scientific/technical uses, with actual availability and access conditions.
- Treat funding as an instrument. A signed partnership does not prove funding,
  transfer or adoption. A patent filing does not establish a commercial result.
- Distinguish delivered work, available capabilities, projects in development,
  intended benefits, measured effects and the 2035 ambition. The proposed
  2026–2035 convention remains a proposal unless its status is separately confirmed.
- Document use of analysis, knowledge, skills and technologies. Completed work
  provides lessons for later programmes; commercialisation is not a universal goal.
- Date and scope figures consistently. Preserve existing owner-supplied figures;
  their reporting periods and evidence remain part of final editorial review.
- Keep partner attribution, scientific rigour and limitations visible. Do not
  manufacture evidence, new service availability, individual experts or guarantees.
